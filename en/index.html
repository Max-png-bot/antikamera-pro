1Lens glare

Your phone's flash makes a camera lens retro-reflect — it glints back in sync with the flash. Move the phone slowly; the app flags glints that blink in rhythm (glossy surfaces don't sync — a lens does).

2Heat signature

Running electronics are warm. Load a video from a thermal camera (USB-C modules like InfiRay/Topdon/FLIR One or a phone with a built-in one) — the app finds small persistent warm spots, filtering out coffee cups, radiators and people.

FAQ

Instant, borderless, no bank involvement, low TRON network fee (~$1). USDT is a dollar-pegged stablecoin: 9.9 USDT ≈ $9.9.
Does it really work, or is it another scam detector app?
Fair question — most "detector" apps are junk. This one is honest physics: the same flashlight method security experts recommend, automated (pulsed flash + sync filtering), plus optional thermal video analysis. It's free to verify before you pay anything. And it states its limits openly (see below).
What are the honest limits?
A camera that is powered off doesn't glow thermally; a matte or recessed lens may not glint. No phone app is a 100% guarantee — this just makes the two physically sound methods practical. Cross-check: heat + glint in one spot = almost certainly a device.
Does my phone need a thermal camera?
No — the lens glare mode works on any phone with a camera and a flash. Thermal mode is a bonus if you own a USB-C thermal module (from ~$100) or a phone with a built-in thermal camera.
Is it legal?
Checking a room you're staying in for your own privacy is legal in most jurisdictions. The app doesn't intercept signals or hack anything — it only analyses images from your own phone.
