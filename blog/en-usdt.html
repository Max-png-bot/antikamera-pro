How to Buy USDT in 5 Minutes and Pay for the App

A beginner's guide · 2026 · no jargon

AntiCamera PRO is paid in USDT — "digital dollars". If you have never touched crypto, that sounds intimidating. In practice it is five minutes and three steps. Here is the explanation I would give a friend.

What USDT is — in two sentences

USDT is an ordinary dollar in digital form: 1 USDT ≈ $1. It is not "wild bitcoin" — the price is stable. People call it a cryptocurrency only because transfers run on the TRON blockchain — like a regular bank transfer, but instant (1–2 minutes), 24/7, with no bank in the middle. The fee is about $1.

Step 1. Buy USDT (2–3 minutes)

The easiest route is an exchange app or wallet with a "buy with card" option. Registration is by phone number or email:

  • Bybit, OKX, Binance — major exchanges; buying USDT with a card works in most countries, and each has a P2P marketplace where people sell USDT for a plain bank transfer;
  • Trust Wallet / TronLink — simple wallets, some with built-in card purchases via partners;
  • crypto ATMs — cash terminals found in larger cities across Europe and Asia.
💡 P2P is the cheapest route in most countries: you send the seller money from your regular card, they release USDT to you. The exchange escrows the deal — the seller only gets your money after the USDT reaches you.

Step 2. Send USDT to the app's wallet (1 minute)

  1. Open the AntiCamera PRO purchase page and press "Copy" next to the wallet address (or scan the QR code straight from your wallet app);
  2. In your wallet tap "Send" → paste the address → amount 6.9 USDT;
  3. Select the TRC20 network — this matters! (sometimes called "TRON");
  4. Double-check the address by eye: starts with TBA8d1e, ends with q7Nzrc9;
  5. Send.
⚠️ The two classic beginner mistakes: (1) sending on the wrong network — ERC20 or BEP20. The money goes "nowhere retrievable"; recovery is rarely possible. You need TRC20 in the network list. (2) Sending less than required — the code is not issued until the difference is paid. Re-check the amount before sending.

Step 3. Copy the transaction hash (30 seconds)

After sending, open the transfer details in your wallet — there is a "Hash" / "TxID" / "Transaction ID" line of 64 characters (letters and digits). Copy it whole and paste it into the "Transaction hash" field on the purchase page. The server verifies the payment on the blockchain itself and shows your activation code instantly.

Is it legal in your country? — the 2026 summary

We only describe lawful routes. Below is the buy/sell status for major jurisdictions (as of November 2026). This is general information, not legal advice — laws change.

🇺🇸 United States

Legal and regulated. The GENIUS Act (2025) governs stablecoins; market-structure law (CLARITY Act) is in progress. Buy on KYC exchanges (Coinbase, Kraken, Gemini). Crypto is taxed as property: capital gains 0–37% depending on holding period and income — keep records.

🇪🇺 European Union

MiCA regulation is in full force across all 27 member states: buy only from CASP-licensed operators (check your national regulator's register). Taxes vary by country — Germany: tax-free after a 12-month hold; EU average 25–30% on gains. Travel Rule data requirements apply from 2026.

🇬🇧 United Kingdom

Legal via FCA-registered platforms; crypto promotion is strictly regulated. Capital gains tax applies.

🇯🇵 Japan · 🇰🇷 South Korea · 🇭🇰 Hong Kong · 🇸🇬 Singapore

  • Japan: legal, FSA-licensed exchanges only (bitFlyer, Coincheck, GMO); ~20% flat tax from 2026;
  • Korea: legal, exchanges require real-name bank accounts (Upbit, Bithumb); ~20% on gains over 2.5M KRW;
  • Hong Kong: legal, 12 SFC-licensed VATP platforms; retail trading permitted; no capital gains tax;
  • Singapore: legal, MAS-licensed operators; capital gains generally exempt.

🇦🇪 UAE · 🇹🇷 Turkey · 🇮🇳 India

UAE (Dubai): VARA-licensed exchanges; no personal income tax. Turkey: legal via MASAK-registered exchanges (BTCTurk, Binance TR). India: legal since 2020 but heavily taxed — flat 30% on gains plus 1% TDS per transaction; use FIU-registered exchanges.

🇧🇷 Brazil · 🇲🇽 Mexico · 🇦🇷 Argentina

Brazil: legal, exchanges registered with the central bank (Binance BR, Mercado Bitcoin, Foxbit); 15–22.5% tax on gains above the threshold. Mexico: legal under the fintech law (Bitso). Argentina: legal, CNV-registered platforms.

🇹🇭 Thailand · 🇻🇳 Vietnam · 🇹🇼 Taiwan

Thailand: legal via licensed exchanges, 15% tax on profits. Vietnam: ownership not prohibited, no unified framework yet — P2P and exchange purchases are common; follow tax rules on sales. Taiwan: not prohibited; FSC regulation in progress — major international exchanges are available.

⚠️ Where buying is banned or restricted

Full bans (about 10 countries): mainland China, Algeria, Bangladesh, Bolivia, Nepal, Tunisia; restricted: Egypt, Morocco (partial). If you are in one of these, we say it straight: there is no lawful exchange route to buy USDT there, and we do not describe workarounds. Common questions

Is this legal? Yes — in most countries (see the country-by-country summary above); in a handful of jurisdictions buying crypto is banned, and we say so plainly rather than suggest workarounds.

What if I pay and the code doesn't arrive? The delivery bot never sleeps. If something goes wrong (say, the amount was short), write to us — contacts are on the purchase page; we will restore it from your transfer hash.

Does the rate jump around? USDT is pegged to the dollar: 6.9 USDT ≈ $6.9. While you transfer, the rate may shift by fractions of a cent — it does not matter: the required amount is in USDT, not in your local currency.
