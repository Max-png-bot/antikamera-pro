Airbnb Hidden Camera Check: the 5-Minute Arrival Protocol

Protocol · 2026 · 6 min read

You have just walked into a rental. Bags down, lights off, five minutes — that is the whole protocol. It works for hotels too. The order matters: cheap and fast first, everything else after.

Minute 1 — stand where a camera would stand

Stand in the doorway and look at the bed and the bathroom. Those are the two things a voyeur pays to see. Every check you do next is along those sight-lines, nowhere else. A camera pointed at a wall is nobody's camera.

Minute 2 — the six spots that hide 90% of cameras

  1. Smoke detector — extra "eyeball" hole or tilted toward the bed? Real ones sit flat on the ceiling;
  2. Charger / plug adapter you did not bring, aimed at the bed;
  3. Alarm clock, radio, speaker near the bed — look for a pinhole "pupil";
  4. Mirror — fingertip test: a 3–5 mm gap between fingertip and reflection is a normal mirror; "touching" means a possible two-way mirror;
  5. Curtain rod, vents, coat hooks, towel racks, plush toys, picture frames above the bed and in the bathroom;
  6. Wires that lead nowhere — power has to reach the device.

Minute 3 — flashlight, or better, the flash of your phone

Lights off, curtains drawn. Sweep the beam slowly along the six spots. A camera lens returns a sharp point of light right back at your eyes — retro-reflection, the same effect as cat eyes at night. Gloss and chrome give a smeared glare instead.

The human eye is the weak link: in a dim room, glints are easy to miss. Minute 4 — dark room, front camera

Still dark: open your phone camera (the front one sees infrared better) and pan around. Night-vision cameras betray themselves as faint red-violet dots — their IR illuminators. This catches night-vision devices and only them.

Minute 5 — Wi-Fi scan

Join the rental's Wi-Fi and run a network scanner (Fing). Unknown devices named like "IP-CAM" are a red flag. Limit: a camera with its own SIM or one recording to an SD card never appears here — which is why minutes 1–3 matter more.

If you actually find one

  1. Do not touch or unplug it — it is evidence;
  2. Photograph and film it from all angles, including where it stood;
  3. Leave its field of view; call the host or hotel — demand a different room and a refund;
  4. File with the police: covert recording in private areas is a crime in most jurisdictions (see our hidden camera laws guide);
  5. Report to the platform: Airbnb's policy bans hidden cameras in private spaces — they refund and rehouse guests.
