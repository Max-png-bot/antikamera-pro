Hidden Camera Laws 2026: Is a Camera in Your Hotel or Rental Illegal?

Country guide · 2026 · 8 min read · not legal advice

Short answer: in almost every developed country, a hidden camera in the place where you sleep or bathe is a crime — while a camera in a hallway or lobby is usually legal. The dividing line is a legal concept worth knowing: "reasonable expectation of privacy".

The one rule that decides most cases

If a reasonable person would expect privacy somewhere — a bedroom, a bathroom, a changing room, a rented apartment — covert recording there is criminal almost everywhere. Where no one expects privacy (a hotel corridor, a reception, a street), cameras are generally permitted. This is why "there was a camera in the smoke detector above the bed" is a police matter, and "there was a camera in the lobby" is not.

Country by country (short version)

  • United States — federal video voyeurism statute (18 U.S.C. § 1801) criminalizes recording of private areas without consent; state laws add their own, stricter ones. Airbnb's own policy bans hidden cameras in private spaces outright — hosts have faced millions in judgments and arrests (the 2024–2026 arrest waves of hosts secretly filming guests made national news).
  • European Union — GDPR treats covert recording in private spaces as unlawful processing of personal data, on top of each country's criminal code (e.g., Germany's § 201a StGB — up to two years for violating intimate privacy by images).
  • United Kingdom — voyeurism offences under the Sexual Offences Act 2003, extended to cover recording in private spaces; the ICO also prosecutes unlawful surveillance.
  • Japan — 2023 law explicitly criminalized "dense shooting" (盗撮) — secret photography in private spaces, train groping-style photography included; arrests in hotels and changing rooms are routine.
  • South Korea — the strictest after the "molka" (hidden-camera) crisis of 2018: special statutes on filming and distribution, hotel "spycam" scandals led to nationwide crackdowns and a mandatory inspection regime for accommodations.
  • China — secret filming in private spaces violates the Personal Information Protection Law and criminal law; a wave of hotel-pinhole prosecutions in 2023–2025 made inspections routine.
  • UAE / Gulf — strict privacy laws; several high-profile prosecutions of hosts and even guests recording roommates. Penalties include jail and deportation.

Details differ, the direction is identical: a lens pointed at your bed or shower is on the wrong side of the law in every jurisdiction listed.

What this means for you as a guest

  1. You are entitled to a camera-free private space in paid accommodation — demanding another room and a refund upon finding one is normal, not hysterical;
  2. Do not touch or unplug the device — it is evidence; photograph and film it first;
  3. Report to the police in that country; in rentals, also to the platform (Airbnb/Booking ban hidden cameras in private areas and refund + rehouse guests);
  4. For civil claims (compensation), your photos + the police report are the foundation.
