How to Find a Hidden Camera in a Hotel Room or Airbnb: 2026 Field Guide

Field guide · updated 2026 · 7 min read

Hidden cameras in rentals and hotel rooms are a real, documented problem — from Seoul guesthouses to Dublin Airbnbs. The good news: you don't need a $300 detector. You need your phone, ten minutes, and this checklist.

1. Think like the installer

A camera has to see something private. Stand in the middle of the room: where would you hide a camera to film the bed or the shower? Those spots get checked first. A camera pointed at a wall is nobody's camera.

2. The "doesn't belong" scan

  • Smoke detectors — real ones sit flat on the ceiling; fake ones have an extra "eyeball" hole or tilt toward the bed;
  • Chargers and plug adapters you didn't bring — especially ones aimed at the bed;
  • Alarm clocks, radios, speakers — look for a tiny lens "pupil" hole;
  • Mirrors — the finger test: on a normal mirror your fingertip and its reflection have a 3–5 mm gap; on a two-way mirror they "touch";
  • Curtain rods, vents, coat hooks, towel racks, stuffed toys, picture frames above the bed and in the bathroom;
  • Wires that lead nowhere — power still has to reach the device.

3. The flashlight trick — the core method

Lights off, curtains drawn. Sweep a flashlight slowly across every suspicious spot and watch reflections: a camera lens retro-reflects a sharp point of light back at you — the same effect as cat eyes at night. Glossy plastic and chrome give a smeared glare instead. This is the method security experts recommend. Its weakness is the human eye: in a dim room, glints are easy to miss — which is where automation helps (step 4).

4. Automate it: blinking-flash detection

The AntiCamera PRO web app (free, runs in your phone's browser) pulses the flash in a rhythm and flags only glints that blink in sync with the flash — chrome and table gloss don't sync, a lens does. A confirmed find gets a red box, vibration and sound. Works on any phone with a flash; all processing stays on the device.

5. Red dots: night-vision check

In total darkness, open your phone camera (front camera sees IR better) and look around: infrared illuminators appear as faint red/violet dots. This catches night-vision cameras — and only them.

6. Wi-Fi sweep

Join the room's Wi-Fi and run a network scanner (Fing or similar). Unknown "IP-CAM"-style devices are a red flag. Limitation: a camera on its own SIM or recording to an SD card never shows up here.

7. The kill test

Unplug everything in the room. Did a "sensor" die with the power? It was mains-powered electronics. If you own a USB-C thermal camera (InfiRay, Topdon, FLIR One, from ~$100), thermal video analysis finds small persistent warm spots — powered electronics — while filtering out coffee cups, radiators and people.

If you actually find one

  1. Don't touch or unplug it — document it with photos and video from all angles;
  2. Step out of its view; call the host/hotel management — demand a different room and a refund;
  3. For rentals: police. Covert recording in private areas is a crime in most jurisdictions.
📱 Honest iOS note: browsers on iOS (all of them, including Safari) do not let a web page control the phone's flash — that is an Apple restriction, not an app bug. The fix is simple: turn on the flashlight from Control Center (swipe from the top-right corner) — the app automatically switches to continuous mode and catches glints from your flashlight. On Android the flash pulses automatically.
